# Φ011 — Independent perception

## Question

Does combining three distinct spatial observations improve estimation over three repeated observations of one sensor? Under which fault structures does the benefit disappear?

Nine entities observe a hidden binary state for 36 rounds. Only the evaluator knows ground truth and sensor fault assignments. Three fixed estimators receive matched worlds: a single local reading, majority over three local readings, or majority over one local and two ring-neighbor readings. The three-reading variants have equal per-decision input counts, but different message and total sensor acquisition costs. No ground-truth labels are supplied to the estimators.

## Regimes

- Independent: each reading flips independently with probability 0.15.
- Persistent local fault: three randomly selected sensors invert every reading, in addition to independent noise. Their identities remain fixed within a world.
- Shared fault: a common flip occurs with probability 0.3 per round, followed by independent noise.

The default report contains 90 seeds per regime, 270 worlds, and 87,480 predictions per estimator. Report mean accuracy, mean worst-entity accuracy, paired accuracy differences and observation/message counts. A network is not guaranteed to improve results: clustered faults and common-mode errors can defeat majority voting. No statistical significance claim is made.

## Limits and next gate

The ring and aggregation rule are supplied, not learned. The simulator holds the complete sample array, and estimators access designated entries by convention; this is not a real isolation boundary. Neither messages nor sender identities are authenticated. No actual bandwidth, latency, asynchronous delivery, learning, or adaptive trust is modeled. The baseline controls readings per decision, not total sensing energy or message cost. The experiment investigates distributed estimation, not consciousness, general intelligence or independent moral reasoning.

Next: expose each learner only to its own local state and a bounded mailbox, introduce held-out changes, learn when to request and trust evidence, and connect decisions to the fixed Φ010 authorization kernel.
