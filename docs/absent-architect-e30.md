# Absent Architect E3.0 — voluntary exchange

Standalone synthetic successor to E2.5 inspired by Phi010. No actual economy, autonomous consciousness, physical cosmology, real-world control or cryptographic authorization claim. Earlier experiments untouched.

## Design
Nine residents, two integer goods A and B, 36 sequential proposal opportunities, 90 deterministic paired seeds. Utility per resident = min(A,B), aggregate sum; complementary goods are useful together. Balanced initial inventory 6/6; specialized/adversarial 10/2, 2/10 and 6/6 repeated by resident ID; shock is a changed **initial allocation** (A adjusted at first six residents, no mid-run shock; total may differ from specialized and is conserved from that fixture's initial state). Compare policies within each regime only. Actor and counterpart schedule matched across policies; exact terms adapt to each policy's current balances, hence not identical proposals across policies. Scripted actor chooses surplus good (B on tie), asks for the other, one or two units. Both goods exchange in one atomic transaction.

Simulator-bound actor must own the offered good. Exact quote includes parties, nonce, goods, amount and kind. Scripted consent only if funded and principal's utility strictly increases. Bilateral requires both grants; sender-only deliberately omits recipient consent; isolated refuses transfers. Every third adversarial opportunity suppresses recipient consent, next impersonates sender (actual actor differs); normal otherwise. No credential forgery, real identities or real adversarial execution. Grants generated by trusted simulator; execute assumes authentic grant objects, does not authenticate arbitrary caller input. Nonce consumed only on successful transaction; replay rejects repeated committed nonce. No market price discovery, coercion dynamics, debt, currency, learning, external access or actual process isolation. Each refusal leaves both inventories unchanged. Ledger replay validates recorded transitions, not grant authenticity or whole-history forgery.
